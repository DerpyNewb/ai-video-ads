30 s · 16:9 · YouTube and web

Mistline Coffee

Seven Veo 3.1 Fast clips from Google Flow, each starting from an approved still so the bag and the light match from shot to shot. Voiceover by Gemini TTS, graded with a custom misty-morning LUT and finished in DaVinci Resolve.

  • An arm crosses the frame in the coffee-picking shot. Stepped through at 4 frames a second, it stays one arm, and every hand keeps five fingers.
  • Veo's own sound had a snap that peaked near full scale over the voice. Clip sound now sits under the voice at a fixed level.
Voiceover
  1. Up here, the day starts above the clouds.
  2. Every cherry is picked by hand.
  3. Roasted slowly, in small batches.
  4. Grown on small farms in the Benguet highlands.
  5. Mistline Coffee.

15 s · 16:9 · YouTube and web

Lumaro Sparkling Calamansi

Four Gemini Omni clips made in the Gemini app, three with the same product still so the can stays the same. Voiceover by Gemini TTS, finished in DaVinci Resolve.

  • Omni sometimes cuts to a new angle inside a clip. Each shot uses only a stretch without a cut.
  • At the tab pop the can shrinks by about a fifth, so that shot ends on the crack.
  • The first super, "Made with real calamansi juice.", ran across the can. It became "Real calamansi."
Voiceover
  1. Calamansi, the Philippine lime.
  2. Lightly sweet, with a sour little bite.
  3. Lumaro Sparkling Calamansi.

12 s · 9:16 for Reels and Stories, plus a 4:5 cut for Feed

Kalan Chili Garlic Crisp

Five Veo 3.1 Fast clips from Google Flow, with a jar still made in Flow so the jar matches. Cut for a feed: something moves in the first frame, the brand is on screen by 2 seconds, and there's a cut every 2 to 3 seconds. It works muted, with Veo's sizzle and crunch as the sound.

  • Captions stay out of the top 14% and bottom 35% of the frame, where Reels and Stories put the account name and buttons, and inside the 4:5 middle that Feed shows.
  • In the spoon shot the jar loses its lid with no one touching it, so the edit uses a later stretch of that clip.

12 s · 1:1 · Facebook and Instagram feed

Dapit Soy Candles

Veo makes only wide and tall video, so the four clips were generated wide with the candle in the centre and cut to the middle square. Slower than the Reels ad, with four 3-second shots. The only sound is the candle's crackle.

  • In the opening shot the match arrived already burnt out, and the flame lit before it touched the wick, so the edit uses the steady flame after it.
  • The third shot's first take had a thicker wick than the first shot. It was generated again to match.
  • The first supers were white on cream wax and hard to read. They now sit on a soft dark gradient.

How each ad is made

  1. Brief

    One page on the product, the audience and the claims the ad may make.

  2. Shot list

    One subject and one camera move per shot, and no on-screen text asked of the model, which garbles it. A script checks the rules and writes the prompts.

  3. Generate

    Veo 3.1 in Google Flow or Gemini Omni in the Gemini app, with a product still attached so the product matches across shots.

  4. Check

    A script rejects clips with black frames, freezes, or the wrong length or shape, and flags cuts hidden inside a clip.

  5. Review

    Each clip is checked frame by frame for hands, faces, garbled text, objects that change shape and physics that look wrong. For these four, Claude Code did that review, and it is recorded as an AI review.

  6. Finish

    The script grades the clips, sets the titles and levels the sound. DaVinci Resolve does the edit and the render, and each ad ships with a record that marks it AI-generated.

These are spec ads for brands that don't exist. Every clip is AI-generated, and none of this is client work.
