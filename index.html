Mistline Coffee

30 seconds · 16:9 · YouTube and the web

Seven Veo 3.1 Fast clips from Google Flow, each one started from an approved still so the bag and the light match from shot to shot. Gemini TTS reads the voiceover, a colour grade gives everything the same misty-morning look, and DaVinci Resolve renders the cut.

  • An arm crosses the frame in the coffee-picking shot. Claude Code stepped through it at 4 frames a second to make sure: it stays one arm, with five fingers on every hand.
  • Veo's own sound had a loud snap in it that jumped over the voice. Now the mix keeps the clip sound under the voice the whole way through.
Voiceover
  1. Up here, the day starts above the clouds.
  2. Every cherry is picked by hand.
  3. Roasted slowly, in small batches.
  4. Grown on small farms in the Benguet highlands.
  5. Mistline Coffee.

Lumaro Sparkling Calamansi

15 seconds · 16:9 · YouTube and the web

Four Gemini Omni clips made in the Gemini app, three of them using the same product still so the can doesn't change between shots. Gemini TTS reads the voiceover, and DaVinci Resolve renders the cut.

  • Omni sometimes cuts to a new angle partway through a clip, so each shot only uses a stretch with no cut in it.
  • When the tab pops, the can shrinks by about a fifth. That shot ends right on the crack.
  • The first on-screen line, "Made with real calamansi juice.", ran across the can. Claude Code trimmed it to "Real calamansi."
Voiceover
  1. Calamansi, the Philippine lime.
  2. Lightly sweet, with a sour little bite.
  3. Lumaro Sparkling Calamansi.

Kalan Chili Garlic Crisp

12 seconds · 9:16 for Reels and Stories, with a 4:5 cut for Feed

Five Veo 3.1 Fast clips from Google Flow, with a jar still made in Flow so the jar stays the same. It's paced for a feed: something moves in the first frame, the brand's on screen by 2 seconds, and there's a cut every 2 to 3 seconds. It works with the sound off, too.

  • Captions stay out of the top 14% and the bottom 35% of the frame, where Reels and Stories put the account name and buttons. They also stay inside the middle part that Facebook Feed shows.
  • In the spoon shot the jar loses its lid with nobody touching it, so the edit uses a later stretch of that clip.

Dapit Soy Candles

12 seconds · 1:1 · Facebook and Instagram feed

Veo only makes wide and tall video, so it made the four clips wide with the candle in the middle, and the edit keeps the centre square. It's slower than the Reels ad: four shots, 3 seconds each.

  • The opening shot shows the steady flame, not the lighting. In the clip the match showed up already burnt out, and the flame lit before it touched the wick.
  • The third shot's first take had a thicker wick than the first shot, so Veo made it again from a corrected prompt.
  • The titles sit on a soft dark gradient, because white type on cream wax was hard to read.

Tamis Ube Jam

12 seconds · 1:1 · Facebook and Instagram feed

Stop-motion claymation from Veo 3.1 Fast in Google Flow, built on clay stills that Nano Banana made in Flow. The titles are in a handwritten face to match.

  • Halfway through the spreading shot, the clip shatters into tiles and jumps to a new scene. The edit keeps only the clean second half.
  • After the lid pops off in the first shot, a second lid shows up next to the jar, so that shot ends just before it does.
  • Veo copied the framing of the jar still it was given, which put the end title on the lid. Twice. A wider still with the jar low in the frame fixed it on the third take.

Habi Rattan Bag

15 seconds · 9:16 for Reels and Stories, with a 4:5 cut for Feed

A creator-style phone video. The woman is AI-generated, and Veo 3.1 Fast voiced her lines right in the clips. The captions repeat what she says, for anyone watching with the sound off.

  • Veo made the phone shot twice. The first take turned the round bag into a half-moon with a leather flap.
  • Veo paced the lines its own way: a pause before "bag", and a long one before "it all fits". A Whisper transcript gave the timing of every word, so every cut falls between words. The phone shot ends before "it all fits", and its caption just says "Phone, wallet, keys."
  • Veo made the end shot again from further back. In the first take, the peg and the top of the strap sat right where the brand name goes.
Lines
  1. Okay, I need to show you this bag.
  2. Phone, wallet, keys.
  3. Handwoven, and it goes with everything.

Init Salabat

20 seconds · 16:9 · YouTube and Facebook

A short story with dialogue: a grandmother and her grandson on a rainy afternoon in Baguio, both AI-generated. Veo 3.1 Fast voiced every line in the clips, and the music sits under them.

  • Veo put a third hand in the pouring shot, and the review missed it because it only looked at about one frame a second. The second take did the same once the kettle came in. So the shot now ends on the spoonful, before the kettle, and "Hot water" is gone.
  • In the last shot Veo threw in two lines that weren't in the script. They stayed: "It'll warm you up" is what the brief wanted the ad to say anyway, and it makes no health claim.
  • Flow Music can split a track into stems. The first track for this ad had something in its vocal stem from 16 seconds on, which could have been a voice or just the cello. Claude Code swapped in a second take whose vocal stem is silent apart from one faint blip.

  3. Better than my jacket.
  4. Smells good, Lola.
  5. It'll warm you up, Apo.

How each ad gets made

  1. Every ad starts from a one-page brief: the product, who it's for, and the claims the ad is allowed to make.
  2. A coding agent turns the brief into a shot list, with one subject and one camera move per shot. It never asks the video model for on-screen text, because the model garbles it. A script checks the shot list against those rules and writes the prompts.
  3. Veo 3.1 in Google Flow, or Gemini Omni in the Gemini app, makes each shot, with a still of the product or the people attached so they look the same every time. When Veo speaks a line itself, a Whisper transcript gives each word's timing, so every cut lands between words.
  4. The script throws out clips with black frames, freezes, or the wrong length or shape, and flags cuts hidden inside a clip.
  5. Claude Code goes through frames from every clip looking for bad hands and faces, garbled text, objects that change shape and physics that look off, and logs what it finds as an AI review.
  6. Flow Music makes each ad's music from a short brief, and its stem split flags anything that might be a voice.
  7. The script grades the clips, sets the titles and mixes the sound, with the music under any voice and up front when nobody's talking. DaVinci Resolve renders the edit, and every ad ships with a record that marks it as AI-generated.

These are spec ads for brands that don't exist. Every clip is AI-generated, people included, and so is the music. None of it is client work.
