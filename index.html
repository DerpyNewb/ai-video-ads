Mistline Coffee

30 seconds · 16:9 · YouTube and the web

Seven Veo 3.1 Fast clips from Google Flow, each starting from an approved still so the bag and the light match from shot to shot. Gemini TTS reads the voiceover, a colour grade gives every shot the same misty-morning look, and DaVinci Resolve renders the cut.

  • An arm crosses the frame in the coffee-picking shot. Claude Code stepped through it at 4 frames a second: it stays one arm, and every hand keeps five fingers.
  • Veo's own sound included a loud snap that jumped over the voice, so the mix turns the clip sound down under the voice.
Voiceover
  1. Up here, the day starts above the clouds.
  2. Every cherry is picked by hand.
  3. Roasted slowly, in small batches.
  4. Grown on small farms in the Benguet highlands.
  5. Mistline Coffee.

Lumaro Sparkling Calamansi

15 seconds · 16:9 · YouTube and the web

Four Gemini Omni clips made in the Gemini app, three with the same product still so the can looks the same throughout. Gemini TTS reads the voiceover and DaVinci Resolve renders the cut.

  • Omni sometimes cuts to a new angle partway through a clip, so each shot uses only a stretch without a cut.
  • At the tab pop the can shrinks by about a fifth, so that shot ends on the crack.
  • The first on-screen line, "Made with real calamansi juice.", ran across the can, so Claude Code shortened it to "Real calamansi."
Voiceover
  1. Calamansi, the Philippine lime.
  2. Lightly sweet, with a sour little bite.
  3. Lumaro Sparkling Calamansi.

Kalan Chili Garlic Crisp

12 seconds · 9:16 for Reels and Stories, with a 4:5 cut for Feed

Five Veo 3.1 Fast clips from Google Flow, with a jar still made in Flow so the jar looks the same throughout. Paced for a feed: something moves in the first frame, the brand is on screen by 2 seconds, and there's a cut every 2 to 3 seconds. It works with the sound off, and Veo's sizzle and crunch are the soundtrack.

  • Captions stay out of the top 14% and bottom 35% of the frame, where Reels and Stories put the account name and buttons, and inside the middle part that Facebook Feed shows.
  • In the spoon shot the jar loses its lid with no one touching it, so the edit uses a later stretch of that clip.

Dapit Soy Candles

12 seconds · 1:1 · Facebook and Instagram feed

Veo makes only wide and tall video, so it generated the four clips wide with the candle in the centre, and the edit keeps the middle square. Slower than the Reels ad, with four 3-second shots. The only sound is the candle's crackle.

  • In the opening shot the match arrived already burnt out, and the flame lit before it touched the wick, so the edit uses the steady flame after it.
  • The third shot's first take had a thicker wick than the first shot, so Veo generated it again from a corrected prompt.
  • White titles on the cream wax were hard to read, so they now sit on a soft dark gradient.

How each ad is made

  1. Each ad starts from a one-page brief: the product, the audience, and the claims the ad may make.
  2. A coding agent turns the brief into a shot list, with one subject and one camera move per shot, and never asks the video model for on-screen text, which it garbles. A script checks the shot list against those rules and writes the prompts.
  3. Veo 3.1 in Google Flow, or Gemini Omni in the Gemini app, generates each shot, with a still of the product attached so it looks the same in every shot.
  4. The script rejects clips with black frames, freezes, or the wrong length or shape, and flags cuts hidden inside a clip.
  5. Claude Code reviews every clip frame by frame for hands, faces, garbled text, objects that change shape and physics that look wrong, and records it as an AI review.
  6. The script grades the clips, sets the titles and levels the sound. DaVinci Resolve renders the edit, and each ad ships with a record that marks it AI-generated.

These are spec ads for brands that don't exist. Every clip is AI-generated, and none of this is client work.
